Poolside · UX review · May 2026

The voice is right. The information architecture is upside-down.

A direct, opinionated review of six Poolside surfaces across the three audiences — members, club admins, and the platform operator. Pre-launch posture: break what needs breaking.

Stage: Pre-launch / pilot Screens reviewed: 6 of ~25 Posture: Direct, no hedging Output: for Claude Code

§1If you only fix three things per audience

Each list is ordered. The first one is the one you skip at your own peril.

For Members

Three things

  1. Land them on what's overdue, not what's possible. The unpaid-dues pill is the most important pixel and it's a footnote — replace the 5-button hero with a single CTA.
  2. Stop greeting people by their username. "Hi, sjfa!" is a login handle. Use first name or skip it.
  3. Pick desktop or don't. Right now the member surface is a 600px column floating in a 1440px sea. Commit to a real desktop layout, or design mobile-only.
For Club Admins

Three things

  1. Collapse the top nav from 12 tabs to 5: Home · People · Calendar · Money · Messages · Club info. The current grid is a memory test.
  2. Make every form auto-save. "Save changes" on a 6-input form is a forgetting tax — the treasurer will lose work.
  3. Replace the dashboard stat grid with the action queue. They land here because something needs doing, not to admire numbers.
For Super-admin

Three things

  1. Show money and risk, not vanity counts. MRR · MRR Δ7d · Failed payments · At-risk clubs · Trials ending <7d.
  2. Densify the tenants table 2×. Sortable headers, filter chips, ⌘K search, columns the operator triages on (MRR, last login, failed payments, health).
  3. Drop the friendliness padding. "Welcome back!", "1 club ever", emoji headers — strip every line of explainer copy.

§2Per-audience executive summary

Three audiences, three different products, three different definitions of "done."

Members
3/10
Right tone, wrong IA

The serif headlines, the warm navy hero, the "Pool closed for chemicals" event copy — these are good. It feels like a pool club, not a SaaS. But the home screen is laid out around what the system can do (5 actions in a hero), not what the member needs to do.

The most consequential fact on the page (DUES UNPAID) is rendered as a 12px pill, and there's no "Pay" button anywhere on the screen. A 45-year-old mom on her phone will not succeed at the most important member task. Voice is keepable; layout has to be redrawn around the action she came for.

Club admins
4/10
Voice right, IA upside-down

The hint copy ("shown on the home page", "if you have one") is exactly the warm, plain-English tone this audience needs — keep that writer. But the navigation is a maze: 12 top tabs, 10 sub-tabs under Members alone, with labels (Wizard, Impact, Status) that mean nothing to a board treasurer.

The dashboard buries the action queue under vanity stats. The settings form requires manual saving. Forms have inconsistent jargon ("eyebrow", "tier", "default city"). The bones of a friendly admin product are here; the IA needs to be torn up and rebuilt on a 5-tab spine.

Super-admin
5/10
Right instinct, wrong execution

Sidebar instead of top tabs, table-with-actions instead of card-with-padding, impersonation banner working — these are correct calls. But it's still designed like a club admin surface: chrome around tables, friendliness copy, zero risk signals, no MRR, no sort/filter.

For an operator triaging 50 clubs this is a list of names, not a triage console. The fix is mostly subtractive — strip chrome, copy, padding — plus add the three signals (money, risk, recency) that aren't there at all.

§6What's missing entirely

Member surface

  • A clear "I forgot my magic link, send another" flow. Sign-in friction was your stated #1 enemy.
  • Guest pass — appears in your task list but no surface for it.
  • A "Help me, something's wrong" path that doesn't require knowing the admin's email.
  • Push / SMS notification preferences. Members will absolutely sign up for pool-closure alerts.

Admin surface

  • Audit log per household ("who changed what when").
  • Bulk import (CSV) for migrating from the spreadsheet they're currently using.
  • A "season ended" mode that gracefully archives the year's data.
  • Export-to-CSV exists on Households (good); needs to exist on Payments and every list.

Super-admin surface

  • Impersonation audit log — cannot ship without this.
  • MRR breakdown by plan + cohort retention — sparkline-shaped, on the dashboard.
  • Health column / page per club, computed from failed payments × admin-login-recency × member-login-recency × trial-end proximity.
  • "Send a note to all club admins" broadcast tool. Outages and feature ships will need this.

§7Two redesign mocks

The two screens with the highest leverage. Admin dashboard sets the tone for the whole admin surface; super-admin dashboard is furthest from where it needs to be — densifying it unblocks a clear pattern for the rest of the operator UI.

Admin dashboard — action queue as hero

5-tab IA. "What needs you" promoted to hero with one-click bulk resolves and concrete amounts. Vanity stats demoted to a right-rail summary. Setup wizard becomes a checklist that hides when complete.

The member home needs redesign too, but its fix is dominated by the IA + copy moves above; less novel structure to demonstrate.

§8Notes for Claude Code

Implementation notes that aren't UX but will block you if you skip them

  • Audit-log model first. Every "Mark paid", every impersonation, every settings auto-save needs to write to an audit table. Without this you can't ship undo (4.1) or impersonation safely (6.9).
  • Optimistic UI + undo toast as a shared pattern. Implement once, apply to: Mark paid, Approve application, Send reminder, Archive household, Delete photo.
  • Replace top-tab nav with a route-driven sidebar collapsing to a hamburger on mobile. Once you accept the IA collapse from 12→5, the tab pattern stops scaling anyway (5 tabs fit; 5 tabs with sub-tabs need a sidebar).
  • Treat the super-admin surface as a separate app. Shares auth and a few primitives; UX rules are inverted (density > whitespace, terseness > explainers). Don't share layout components 1:1 — share tokens.
  • Auto-save infra: debounce 600ms on blur, optimistic state, "Saved · just now" pill that fades after 2s. Same component everywhere.
  • Keyboard shortcuts on super-admin only, behind a useShortcuts(scope: 'platform-admin') hook. Centralize the registry so ? can render the cheat sheet.
