The short version

Pool clubs are run by volunteers. Volunteers don't have time. Volunteer time is worth real money — even if no one's writing them a check. So pricing should reflect the hours you save, not the cost of writing the code.

Our pricing rule: charge a fraction of the value we create. A typical 150-household neighborhood club saves 200+ board hours per year. That's $5,000+ of volunteer time at $25/hour — Pro is $2,400 — under half the time value alone, before counting the dues that land on time instead of in June.

Why the first season is free

There used to be a Free Forever tier capped at 20 households. It sounded generous and mostly wasn't: almost no real pool club is that small, so it advertised a plan you couldn't actually run a club on.

A free first season is the honest version. You get every feature and no household limit for a full season — long enough to open the pool, collect a season of dues, run your parties and swim lessons, and close out. If it saved your treasurer the spring, pick a plan. If it didn't, you've lost nothing but an afternoon of setup, and your data exports on the way out.

We'd rather be judged on a season than on a 14-day trial nobody has time to use in February.

Why no feature paywalls

Most SaaS products gate the good stuff behind higher tiers — "you can't use SMS sign-in unless you upgrade to Pro" — to manufacture upgrade pressure. We hate that. Volunteer boards have enough decisions to make without "do we need to upgrade just to send SMS?"

Every tier gets every feature. The only thing that changes between tiers is how many households you can have. Outgrowing a tier is a good problem — your club is doing well and you have too many members. That's the right time to upgrade.

About the small percentage on member transactions

Every fee we charge — subscription, transaction, gate, the lot — is listed together on one page. This is the short version.

When your members pay through Poolside (via Stripe), we take a small platform fee on top of Stripe's processing rate:

If you stick with Venmo / check for everything, this never applies — you only pay the subscription. The transaction fee covers the actual benefit of using Stripe (auto-charging saved cards, payment plans, instant verification, no chasing members for dues).

About the payment-plan fee

A member who would rather spread dues across the season instead of paying in one go can choose a payment plan. That costs them $4 per payment, capped at $16 for the whole plan — the same way an installment plan works on insurance or a utility bill.

Two things worth being clear about, because this is the one fee a member sees added to their own bill:

  • The club's books don't move. Your installments are the dues, exactly. The fee rides alongside and comes to us, so a club nets the same whether a household pays once or six times.
  • Paying in full avoids it entirely. It's a fee for the convenience, not a fee for paying.

The cap is the important part. Without it, a flat fee across an eight-payment plan would take $32 on $600 of dues — over 5%, which is the sort of number that makes a board tell its members to write checks instead.
